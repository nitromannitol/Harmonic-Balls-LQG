# Section 11 outside crossing locality

The crossing distances in `paper/lqg_harmonic_ball-corrected.tex:3527`–3531
have outside-field measurable **versions**, simultaneously almost surely, for
the pinned whole-plane GFF base metric. The new theorem is
`IsCorrectedPinchConfiguration.exists_outside_crossing_distances` in
`HarmonicBalls/Proofs/Sec11/CrossingMetricLocality.lean`. It uses the exact
manuscript set `closure Q'` for L and retains the full-plane `setDist`.
Its outside sigma algebra is the one generated by zero-integral pairings on
`(closure W)ᶜ`. This is an almost-sure version statement, not literal
measurability of arbitrary chosen representatives.

The assertion at lines 3613–3616 requires more than locality of arbitrary
full-plane point distances. The relevant special distances are localized by
`Steps/Sec11/LocalPathLength.lean:106,129,165`: truncate each path at its
first exit from an open domain, compare path lengths, then take infima. The
reverse inequality follows from monotonicity of the internal metric. For L,
`buffer_Q'` puts `closure Q'` inside Q and `buffer_Q` puts `closure Q`
outside `closure W`. For a₀ use B_δ and for a₁ use B_(2δ), both with the
buffered domain B_(3δ), outside the core. For a₁ the initial segment starts
on the inner sphere and ends at the first outer crossing; no last-inner
crossing is needed because the buffered domain is the whole outer ball.
No geodesic existence is used.

`Steps/Sec11/LocalExtremaRepresentatives.lean:105` uses continuity on endpoint
subtypes and countable dense sets, preserving one common almost-sure equality.
`Proofs/Sec11/PinchOutsideAbsolutePairing.lean:14` recovers the absolute
normalization from the pinned unit-circle average using tests outside the
core; this relies on the geometric radial gap, and does not assert that
absolute pairings are measurable modulo constants for an unpinned field.
The local metric decoder uses Leonardo Bonanno's LatticeProb formalization.

The theorem concerns L, a₀, a₁ only. The remaining parts of the outside-event
claim (the corridor, d₀, and S) have separate existing representatives; this
result alone does not establish a numbered-result closure.
