## Source and quantifiers

[Pfeffer, arXiv:2104.04020v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.04020v3), Definition 1.5 p. 5, Definition 1.6 p. 6, Proposition 1.9 p. 8, proof of Proposition 2.4 pp. 22–23 (especially (2.25)), and proof of Proposition 1.9 p. 38 were read directly. Definition 1.6 says a measurable map from actual distributions with the weak topology into lower semicontinuous extended metrics, then states I–V whenever `h` is a whole-plane GFF plus a continuous function. V fixes `c_r` before a fixed annulus and requires both distances and their inverses tight as `r > 0` varies; (1.7) uses all `δ ∈ (0,1)` and positive rational `r`. Proposition 1.9 concerns those Axiom V constants, with `Q = Q(ξ)` from (1.4), uniformly in all positive `r`. Its exponent is the LFPP background charge, not a graph-distance exponent or `γ/d_γ`.

The source does not explicitly say that the perturbation is deterministic, bounded, independent, or used only at small scales in Definition 1.6. Nor does it explicitly bind `c` across different `h` instances. Read literally, its opening sentence places V under the general-field clause. Its proof of Proposition 2.4 asserts at (2.25) an exact scale-invariance-in-law identity and says it extends the pure GFF case to general `h` using Weyl scaling. For `h = hC + Re`, the transformed perturbation is `r Re`, not `Re`; hence this asserted general-field extension is not established by that argument and is in general incompatible with it. This is a **suspected scope/error in the printed source**, not an approved correction: the pure normalized GFF interpretation is a plausible repair but is not presented here as what the source actually says.

## Annuli and tightness

* `pfefferAcross` (`:51–52`) takes the infimum of the ambient metric between the two circles; this matches the text's boundary-distance reading, not a path constrained to the annulus. Its set and scaling maps still need measurability and equivalence proofs.
* `pfefferAround` (`:54–62`) uses Euclidean-continuous closed paths of finite or infinite `pathLength`, and defines separation by intersection with **every** inner-to-outer path in the plane. The source says “D-distances of closed paths” and separation, without explicitly specifying boundary touching, whether loops must stay in `A`, or a formal separation predicate. Later source p. 27 calls this `D`-length, supporting the length reading. Circle-boundary touching is allowed by the Lean predicate (a loop equal to either boundary passes it); no equality to any intended annulus-contained/separating convention has been proved. This choice is a proof obligation, not an established difference in either direction. The class only tests round annuli; extension to a broader meaning of “Euclidean annulus” must be checked against the source convention.
* The length definition uses weakly monotone finite sequences, not strict partitions; equality with the source partition supremum follows for genuine nonnegative metrics after adjoining endpoints and removing duplicates, but is not established here.
* `PfefferTightAcrossScales` (`:87–89`) uses `P` of an event with no explicit measurability, and values in `ℝ≥0∞`; the law and reciprocal formulations require the random variables/events measurable and almost surely in `(0,∞)`, together with probability and compactness. The inequality forces the latter a.e. for each scale if the other hypotheses hold, but is not automatically a formulation of laws without measurability. `pfefferCircleAverage` (`:46–47`) must be identified with the source circle average including constants, and the sum with `hcirc` established for actual field representatives.

## T1 and T2: explicit counterexample to the general-field route

Assume a nondegenerate, real-valued Euclidean-compatible length LQG metric with `ξ > 0`, exact coordinate change and Weyl scaling, and a normalized whole-plane GFF `hC`. Such metrics are the physical subcritical setting of the source. Set the *deterministic* continuous perturbation `f(z) = Re z`; its average on every circle centered at 0 is 0. Use the two fixed round annuli `A+ = A(3;1,2)` and `A− = A(-3;1,2)`. At scale `r`, their annular regions lie in `Re z ≥ r` and `Re z ≤ -r`, respectively. Any path joining the boundary circles of `r A+` has a subpath crossing that annulus, so Weyl scaling gives

`D_(hC+f)(across r A+) ≥ exp(ξ r) D_hC(across r A+)`.

For the negative annulus, an admissible path inside `r A−` gives

`D_(hC+f)(across r A−) ≤ exp(-ξ r) D_hC(x_r,y_r; r A−)`

for fixed distinct boundary points with a path through the annulus (or a slightly thickened annulus still inside `{Re z ≤ -r/2}`). In a Euclidean-compatible length metric this internal distance is finite: a compact Euclidean path can be covered by finitely many small Euclidean neighborhoods in which short metric paths stay inside the open region. The positive across distance is strictly positive because disjoint compact circles have positive distance in the same compatible metric.

After dividing both sides by `r^(ξ Qγ) exp(ξ hC_r(0))`, exact coordinate change, constant Weyl scaling and **joint** affine GFF invariance show that the two comparison variables have a scale-independent joint law with the first positive and the second finite a.s. (One must transport the entire continuous metric representative, not just one fixed pair; see below.) Consequently the ratio of the two perturbed across distances is at least `exp(2 ξ r)` times a positive random variable whose law is independent of `r`; it tends to infinity in probability. If a single `c_r` made both normalized distances and inverses tight, their ratio would be tight. Contradiction. Thus no `c` at all satisfies the displayed staged Axiom V for this field and all fixed annuli. In particular, exact power normalizers fail. This uses a deterministic perturbation, so merely restricting random dependence does not help. The proof is mathematical evidence and is **not claimed as a Lean-proved counterexample**; all path measurability and representative transfers remain to be formalized. It applies regardless of how the `c` family depends on this field instance.

A bounded continuous random perturbation does allow a different comparison route: a finite random global sup norm bounds the Weyl factor simultaneously at every scale, and probability tail bounds yield tightness without independence. A locally bounded perturbation alone only supplies such control on compact scale ranges, or on small scales for a fixed annulus; it cannot justify all `r > 0`. Neither variant is asserted to be Pfeffer's stated Definition 1.6 without a ruling.

Prove separately: (1) metric topology and finite/positive across and finite around quantities; (2) their measurable continuous-metric representative and spatial pullback laws; (3) constant Weyl scaling including random circle-average shifts; (4) `(1.7)` for an arbitrary real exponent `ξ * lqgQ γ` using a sufficiently large `Λ`; (5) the exact joint-law transport. `IsLQGMetric.weylExponent_pos` (`Proofs/GFF/MetricXiSign.lean:360`) supplies positivity from `0 < γ`; `IsEuclideanLengthMetric.weylScaling_const_mul` (`Steps/Sec11/WeylMetricConstantScaling.lean:91`) and `weylScaling_add_const` (`Steps/Sec11/WeylScalingOrder.lean:43`) are reusable. These are actual proof obligations, not new literature inputs.

`WholePlaneGFFAffineInvariance` (`Proposed/LQG.lean:51–55`) says that for each deterministic nonzero affine map the recentered *pairings and circle averages together* satisfy `IsWholePlaneGFF`; `Defs/GFF.lean:91` leaves non-test pairings unconstrained. Equality in law of admissible observables must be pushed through a measurable metric representative; pointwise metric evaluations and a.e. continuity can supply countable-coordinate reconstruction only after proof. An equality of arbitrary pairing functionals on non-test arguments does not follow; use measurable indistinguishability on test pairings and prove that `𝔇` respects it. The scale-specific null event in `coordChange` (`Defs/LQGMetric.lean:158–160`) is sufficient for equal laws for each scale, but cannot be promoted to one event for all real scales without extra work. For general `f`, the normalized transformed field contains `f(r ·) − circleAverage f r 0`; affine GFF invariance says nothing that makes this family identically distributed or uniformly controlled.

A source-faithful replacement cannot honestly be written using only the current raw carrier: `𝒟'(ℂ)` with weak-Borel structure and the extended lsc metric space with its lower-semicontinuous topology are not supplied by this module.
