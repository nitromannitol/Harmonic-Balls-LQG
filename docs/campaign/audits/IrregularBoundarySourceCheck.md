# Irregular boundary Green convention: source check

**Verdict.** The zero boundary value is a coherent killed-process extension, but it is not generally the interior limit. Nor is the pointwise infimum of the continuous-on-closure competitors necessarily continuous or itself a competitor. The finite-puncture counterexample below disproves that auxiliary identification, even with smooth positive area measure; it does not disprove Lemma 5.8. The corrected paper does not explicitly choose a boundary version at irregular points, and its killed-process wording does not uniquely specify a density at individual boundary points. The frozen pointwise-zero choice is therefore plausible but not source-certified. No numbered-result closure follows.

**Exact objects and source.** Corrected TeX 468–469 defines the killed Brownian Green function on the closed set, but Proposition 2.3, 478–486, asserts zero boundary only for balls. Lines 581–593 define the closed-ball continuous competitors, their pointwise infimum, and the strict noncontact cluster. Lines 1014–1025 assert the general-domain extension and Lemma 5.8; the frozen statement restricts the pole to the domain, while the printed lemma quantifies over all z in ℂ. The appendix's Green-potential continuity, zero boundary, and shift are stated for balls (3523–3544, 3549–3579). Its citation to Armitage–Gardiner Theorem 4.3.8 concerns the Riesz measure of a Green potential, not continuity up to an arbitrary boundary.

The frozen definitions are `HarmonicBalls.supersolutionsOn (μ : Measure ℂ) (U : Set ℂ) (z : ℂ) (t : ℝ) : Set (ℂ → ℝ)` and `HarmonicBalls.leastSupersolutionOn (μ : Measure ℂ) (U : Set ℂ) (z : ℂ) (t : ℝ) (x : ℂ) : ℝ` and `HarmonicBalls.clusterOn (μ : Measure ℂ) (U : Set ℂ) (z : ℂ) (t : ℝ) : Set ℂ` (Draft/Section5.lean:103–115). The first requires `ContinuousOn w (closure U) ∧ LaplacianLE w μ U` and `∀ x ∈ closure U, x ≠ z → -t * greenFunction U z x ≤ w x`; the second is the pointwise real `sInf` of competitor evaluations; the third is `{x ∈ U | x = z ∨ -t * greenFunction U z x < leastSupersolutionOn μ U z t x}`. The frozen `greenFunction` (Defs/Green.lean:70–74) is the logarithmic kernel minus its greatest harmonic minorant for distinct interior points, and zero if either point lies outside U or on the diagonal. The diagonal is explicitly excluded from the obstacle and the pole inserted in the cluster. These are different objects: the Green kernel is the obstacle, while the infimum is an envelope above its negative.

**Boundary calculation and exact counterexample.** The checked theorem `four_disc_union_irregular_boundary` (Proofs/Potential/GreenRegularBoundaryGeometry.lean:706) gives bounded open V, the union of the four unit discs centered at ±1 and ±i, with `0 ∈ closure V`, `0 ∉ V`, `1/2 ∈ V`, and `¬ Tendsto (greenFunction V (1/2 : ℂ)) (𝓝[V] 0) (𝓝 0)`. Its proof uses `punctured_unit_ball_subset_four_discs` (:659) and `not_tendsto_greenFunction_zero_of_punctured_ball_subset` (:681). Nevertheless `greenFunction V (1/2) 0 = 0` by Defs/Green.lean:100. Thus even this finite union of discs disproves the asserted general zero *interior limit*. It does not disprove the zero *point value*.

A sharper explicit envelope example takes D = B(0,1), U = D minus {0}, z = 1/2, μ planar area measure, and any t > 0. The point is polar, so the killed processes in D and U have the same law until exit when started in U, and the interior Green kernels agree. In the normalization of TeX Proposition 2.3, the disc kernel satisfies `G_D(z,x) ≥ c (1 - |x|²)` for some c > 0: the disc formula gives `G_D = (4π)⁻¹ log(1 + (1-|z|²)(1-|x|²)/|x-z|²)`, and `log(1+s) ≥ s/(1+s)` provides a uniform positive c. Choose `0 < ε ≤ min(1/4, tc)` and set `w(x) = -ε(1-|x|²)`. Then Δw = 4ε ≤ 1, w vanishes on ∂D, and w ≥ −tG_D on D off z. Hence w is a full-disc competitor and `v_D(0) ≤ w(0) = −ε < 0`.

The Lean result `leastSupersolutionOn_finset_punctured_disc_eq` (Proofs/Sec5/FinitePunctureCompetitors.lean:91) proves equality of the two pointwise infima at every x ∈ U off z under local finiteness and `VolumeGrowth`; area measure satisfies these. More precisely `tendsto_leastSupersolutionOn_finset_punctured_disc_at_puncture` (Proofs/Sec5/FinitePunctureStrongCompatibility.lean:12) gives the limit `v_D(0) < 0` as x → 0 through U, while `leastSupersolutionOn_eq_zero_of_boundary` (Proofs/Sec5/IrregularLeastSupersolution.lean:19) gives `v_U(0) = 0`. Thus `v_U` is discontinuous on closure U and is not in `supersolutionsOn μ U z t`. This is a concrete counterexample to the generalization of the ball Lemma 3.1 assertion that the infimum is itself a continuous competitor. The general exact criterion is `leastSupersolutionOn_finset_punctured_disc_mem_iff` (Proofs/Sec5/FinitePunctureAdmissibility.lean:95): under its displayed volume-growth hypotheses admissibility holds iff the disc envelope vanishes at every puncture. It is an auxiliary failure, not a counterexample to 5.8; indeed `clusterOn_finset_punctured_disc_eq_sdiff` (FinitePunctureCompetitors.lean:109) proves the punctured cluster is the disc cluster minus the punctures.

**Meaning of the boundary value.** For the killed transition semigroup, a process started at a point outside U is killed immediately, so choosing the kernel zero there is natural; the occupation density is defined only almost everywhere in its integration variable, so its value at an individual boundary point is not fixed by occupation measure alone. For U above, approaching 0 from inside instead gives `G_D(z,0) > 0`; neither choice is a continuous zero extension. Replacing the point value 0 by that interior limit weakens the obstacle constraint at 0, and can change the competitor class (the full-disc least supersolution above is then admissible at 0). For arbitrary irregular sets there need not be a single ordinary boundary limit, so this prescription itself needs a precise definition. The physically relevant weak Dirichlet formulation is zero quasi-everywhere on the boundary, not a blanket claim of ordinary continuity at every boundary point; this does not by itself prove equality with the frozen pointwise envelope.

**Primary sources.** Armitage–Gardiner, *Classical Potential Theory*, [§4.1 and Theorem 4.1.5](https://www2.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~prazak/vyuka/431-XX/lib/AG_cpt04_1-6.pdf) define the interior Green function by the greatest harmonic minorant and give the disc formula; Example 5.1.2, p. 124, identifies singleton sets as polar; Theorems 6.6.5 and 6.6.8 identify boundary regularity through barriers and show irregular boundary points are polar. The paper's cited Theorem 4.3.8, pp. 103–104, identifies the Riesz measure of a potential, not a continuous boundary extension. These facts do not determine the paper's individual irregular-point kernel values or prove the arbitrary-domain obstacle equivalence. Brownian occupation Green kernels are normalized for generator Δ/2, whereas the paper's displayed ΔG = −δ uses half that occupation density; this factor does not affect the boundary conclusions.

**Smallest unresolved bridge.** For the frozen pointwise-zero convention, prove a *derived* comparison at the actual measure and pole: for every bounded open U and `z ∈ U`, when either cluster closure is compactly contained in U, show that the strict noncontact set of `leastSupersolutionOn μ U z t` has the same closure as the source's killed-process obstacle cluster (with an explicitly fixed source boundary version). Its Lean shape is `closure (clusterOn μ U z t) = closure Λsource`, with the source object and its convention first characterized independently; it is not a proposed new input. The existing exhaustion compatibility proves a conditional equality inside the frozen semantics, not that source identification. Alternatively, for an ordinary continuous-zero-boundary argument, restrict to Green-regular U, expressed by `∀ y ∈ frontier U, Tendsto (greenFunction U z) (𝓝[U] y) (𝓝 0)`, and separately establish the required potential continuity and envelope admissibility. This is a sufficient proof route, not a claim that the source restricts Lemma 5.8. No frozen edit or extra literature assumption is proposed.
