# Physical graph exponent: source and endpoint audit

Leonardo Bonanno contributed to the formalization used in this comparison.
This is a source and interface audit, independent of F3; it introduces no
Lean declaration or literature input. **Verdict:** the fixed-pair statement
has a published source, but the required formal identifications have not
been established by the declarations examined here. The diagonal problem
does not disprove the predicate at the distinct points `0` and `1`.

## Published statements and endpoints

* [Ding–Zeitouni–Zhang, arXiv:1807.00422v1, Theorem 1.1, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.00422v1#page=2): for each `γ ∈ (0,2)` there is a deterministic `χ > 0`; for each fixed **distinct** `u, v` in the interior of `[0,1]²` and each `ι > 0`, a field-measurable random constant bounds the graph distance above and below by powers `δ^(-χ∓ι)` for all `δ ∈ (0,1]`. The zero-boundary GFF area measure is used; the finite covering balls have rational centers and mass at most `δ²`. A countable intersection over tolerances yields an almost-sure logarithmic limit for each fixed pair; the theorem does not assert one event for all pairs.
* [Ding–Gwynne, arXiv:1807.01072v3, pp. 3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.01072v3#page=3) set `d_γ = 2/χ`, explain the squared-mass normalization, and state their Theorem 1.1 for distinct interior square points, with full mass-scale limit `1/d_γ` and `d_γ > 2`. Their [Theorem 1.4, p. 6, (1.8)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.01072v3#page=6) states, for the whole-plane GFF with unit-circle average zero, **for each** `z, w ∈ ℂ`, almost surely the full-scale unrestricted point-to-point exponent is `1/d_γ`. This quantifier is pair first, then almost surely; it is not a simultaneous assertion over all pairs. The unrestricted definition, p. 3, uses arbitrary Euclidean balls of mass at most `ε` covering a continuous path.
* The displayed quantifier of (1.8) includes `z = w`. For a locally finite measure with `μ {z} = 0`, a small positive-radius ball covers the constant path and every cover uses at least one ball, so the distance is 1 and the exponent is 0. This is formalized in `HarmonicBalls/Proofs/Sec2/GraphDistanceDiagonal.lean:15,32,39`. Since the source has finite `d_γ > 2`, its diagonal assertion is false as printed. A missing distinctness qualification is a plausible correction, not an author-confirmed erratum. The same page's Theorem 1.5 explicitly says distinct points, although it concerns LFPP and convergence in probability rather than the graph-distance assertion. The singleton `K = {z}` also makes the first equality in the printed (1.9) fail under the same definition: its maximal point-to-point distance is 1; the text allows every compact connected `K`. A nondegeneracy condition is a candidate correction, not a verified erratum.
* [Gwynne–Pfeffer, arXiv:1905.11790, Corollary 1.7, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.11790#page=7) identifies the Hausdorff dimension of an open domain `U` with that same `d_γ` for the associated `γ`-LQG metric of a GFF plus continuous function; `U = ℂ` is the whole-plane case. Their metric is characterized by its LQG metric axioms with `ξ = γ/d_γ` and is unique up to deterministic positive scaling (Definition 1.2 and Theorem 1.3, pp. 5–6). This is an additional metric theorem, not a consequence of an arbitrary positive point-to-point exponent. The corrected harmonic-balls draft lines 424–426 defines its physical `d_γ` through the whole-plane metric Hausdorff dimension and cites this corollary.

## Exact interfaces

`HarmonicBalls/Defs/LQGGraphDistance.lean:16–54` uses finite positive-radius open-ball covers, arbitrary complex centers, a continuous `Path`, mass threshold `ε`, and `ℕ∞` with `⊤` when no finite cover exists. `IsLiouvilleGraphDimension μ z w d` requires `0 < d`, eventual finiteness along `2⁻ⁿ`, and almost-sure *when placed under an AE binder* dyadic convergence of `log D / (n log 2)` to `1/d`. Its definition itself is deterministic. `isLiouvilleGraphDimension_of_fullScale` at :56 converts a positive full-scale limit to this predicate and establishes eventual finiteness; `IsLiouvilleGraphDimension.fullScale` in `Proofs/Sec2/GraphDimensionScaleInterpolation.lean:133` gives the converse. The `ENat.toNat ⊤ = 0` convention makes the explicit finiteness condition material.

`IsWholePlaneLQGDimension γ d` at `Defs/LQGGraphDistance.lean:97` requires a single positive `d` for **every** probability space and every `hC`, `hcirc`, `μ` satisfying `IsWholePlaneGFF` and `IsLQGMeasureOfField P γ 0 hcirc μ`, with an AE event for the fixed endpoints `0, 1`. These endpoints are distinct; no common event for arbitrary pairs is required. `IsLQGMeasureOfField` uses the a.s. locally finite dyadic circle-average vague limit (`Defs/LQG.lean:53–75`), so identification of the paper's measure and the exact representative is a real obligation. `IsWholePlaneGFF` has joint Gaussian observables and a continuous positive-radius circle version (`Defs/GFF.lean:93`).

`lqgGraphDimension` at `Defs/LQGGraphDistance.lean:105` chooses a witness only if one exists; otherwise it is `1`. Its unconditional positivity theorem proves only positivity. The specification at :118 requires `∃ d, IsWholePlaneLQGDimension γ d`; equality with another candidate at :136 additionally uses an actual realization. The staged `WholePlaneLQGGraphDimension` (`Proposed/LQGDimension.lean:19`) asserts such witnesses for `0 < γ < 2`, pending source check; this audit neither accepts nor invokes it.

`SatisfiesStrongMetricAxiomsAtDimension` (`Defs/StrongMetricSpaces.lean:429`) takes a positive candidate `dimension`, imposes metric axioms at `γ / dimension`, and quantifies over represented fields and continuous shifts. `IsStrongLQGMetric` at :444 specializes it to `lqgGraphDimension γ`. An axioms-at-dimension theorem at Bonanno's `LQGMetric.dGamma γ` cannot be substituted there until equality of the dimension choices is proved. Conversely, an arbitrary candidate satisfying an axioms-at-dimension interface should not be called the physical dimension without the required uniqueness and identification bridges.

## Bonanno comparison and required repairs

The operator's `~/paper-fleets/hb/audits/LEO-INPUT-MAP.md`, row 11, records a statement-level match, not a proved exponent existence theorem. In the ported `LatticeProb/Bonanno/LQGMetric/Statement/Dimension.lean:38–62`, `lgdDZZ` uses the zero-boundary unit-square measure, rational centers and mass `δ²`; `IsLGDExponent γ χ` quantifies over each zero-boundary realization and each fixed distinct interior pair before the AE event. `chiDZZ` chooses a **positive** witness when one exists and defaults to `1/2` otherwise; `dGamma = 2 / chiDZZ`, and `xiGamma = γ / dGamma`. The fallback value `dGamma = 4` is not evidence for `IsLGDExponent` or for the physical dimension. The map's absent/unported `DZZChiIdent` is an identification interface, not an unconditional theorem, and a module or `.olean` alone supplies no proof or audited dependency closure.

For an actual comparison, the remaining obligations are:

1. Establish a positive `χ` satisfying Bonanno's exact `IsLGDExponent γ χ`, with the correct zero-boundary measure and fixed-pair AE order, or establish directly the corresponding normalized whole-plane exponent from DG (1.8) at `0,1`. DZZ's source supplies the mathematical claim; the corresponding Lean proof and faithful representation transfer remain open. The source's countable intersection over tolerances must precede the full-scale limit, without moving AE across uncountably many endpoints.
2. Compare square and whole-plane GFFs and their actual GMC representatives on the relevant region, including normalization and positive continuous/harmonic perturbations. Prove the needed restricted-versus-unrestricted path comparison and rational-versus-arbitrary-center cover comparison at exponent level; equal-threshold equality is not automatic, especially at mass exactly equal to the threshold. The existing normalized field bridge `Proofs/GFF/BonannoNormalizedVersion.lean:26,50,97` gives distributional normalization and each fixed circle coordinate a.s., not by itself the required joint GMC/graph-distance identification.
3. Account for the scale algebra: with `ε = δ²`, `log(1/ε) = 2 log(1/δ)`, so the exponent per mass scale is `χ/2 = 1/(2/χ)`. Transfer the full positive-scale limit to `2⁻ⁿ`, preserve finiteness, and produce the **same deterministic** candidate for all project realizations. Once an actual `IsWholePlaneLQGDimension γ (LQGMetric.dGamma γ)` is established, the existing `IsWholePlaneLQGDimension.lqgGraphDimension_eq` plus a concrete valid project realization proves `lqgGraphDimension γ = LQGMetric.dGamma γ`; the choice defaults cannot be used as a substitute.
4. For the paper's physical interpretation, apply/establish the separate Gwynne–Pfeffer Hausdorff theorem for the associated physical metric and transport it through the metric map, carrier, topology and probability-law interfaces, including the irrelevant positive global metric normalization. Separately translate Bonanno's strong metric interface and all representation/measurability and continuous-shift quantifiers to `SatisfiesStrongMetricAxiomsAtDimension`. The numerical equality alone does not prove either the metric interface or Hausdorff dimension for an arbitrary metric.
